Image-based detection of salt stress in hydroponic tomatoes

Type
NSF REU, University of Wyoming
Advisor
Prof. Liping Wang
Output
GreenSys2027 abstract, first author
Status
Abstract submitted

I built this pipeline on my own during an NSF REU with Prof. Liping Wang at the University of Wyoming, working from photos the lab had already taken of tomato plants grown hydroponically at different salt levels. The goal was to look at a photo of a plant and estimate how much salt it had been given, which is a step toward screening which plant varieties handle salt best.

In more technical terms:

YOLOv8 segments each plant's shoot, and then colour, texture and shape features are normalized to untreated controls of the same accession before ridge regression with grouped cross-validation predicts salinity dose. Across 149 plants, four accessions and eight salinity levels from 0 to 50 dS/m, the predictions reached a Spearman correlation of 0.921 with dose, and intensity features alone reached 0.903.

Background brightness tracked the dose. Background brightness in the images correlated with salinity dose because lighting differed between treatment groups, so part of what looks like stress signal may actually be illumination, which the abstract flags as a confounder.

0.991 hides the part that matters. Overall mask mAP50 was 0.991, but segmentation error was worst on injured tissue, which is the tissue that decides the score, so the aggregate number hides where the model is weakest.

The abstract was submitted to GreenSys2027 with me as first author and is under review.
