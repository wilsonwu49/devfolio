LiDAR view synthesis

Type
Research, ASAR Lab
Advisor
Prof. Jason Rife
Status
Complete

Undergraduate research in Prof. Jason Rife's ASAR Lab at Tufts. Given two LiDAR frames captured from different positions, the goal is to synthesize what the scene looks like from the reference frame using data from the new frame, which lets you compare scans taken from arbitrary sensor poses, and I scored each method by the RMSE between the synthesized and ground-truth range images.

  • Built a full MATLAB benchmarking pipeline running 6 interpolation methods across 60-frame datasets with ICP pose registration
  • Methods compared: bilinear splatting with Gaussian hole filling, bilinear splat only, nearest neighbor, linear, triangle warp, and FFT lookup
  • Designed a novel FFT-based method evaluating fractional IDFT at pose-transformed reference pixel positions, eliminating scatter and gridding entirely
  • Results evaluated using RMSE, median error, 95th percentile error, and coverage vs pose distance
  • Data collected on Ouster OS2-128 (128x2048) from urban driving at Volpe National Transportation Systems Center

The main finding was that interpolation error isn't random, since each method fails in a structured, scene-dependent way at depth discontinuities, although benchmarking like this gives an empirical characterization rather than certified bounds.
