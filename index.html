LiDAR autonomous navigation robot

Type
Personal project, built alone
Hardware
RPLIDAR, STM32L432KC, Raspberry Pi 4
Status
Simulation only
Open full write-up

An autonomous ground robot I'm building on my own around an RPLIDAR, an STM32 and a Raspberry Pi, with a navigation stack written from scratch in Python that runs from scan-matching ICP and a 15-state error-state EKF through a log-odds occupancy grid, an A* planner, pure pursuit tracking and a control barrier function (CBF) safety filter. I started it to get hands-on experience with LiDAR and taught myself the estimation and control along the way through study guides I wrote as I went, and so far the whole stack has been validated in simulation rather than on hardware.

A negative result: making the CBF safety margin grow with the filter's uncertainty did not help in simulation, and it may have made things worse.

Currently working on bringing it up on hardware.

Hardware

Status

A semantically aware safe control system in Prof. Ryan Cosner's SPARC Lab, where the robot has to avoid hazards that LiDAR can't see, like caution tape, and still respect them after they leave the camera's field of view, since the object and semantic maps start to degrade once that happens. It runs on a Go2 robot dog, and I'm adding to an existing project, working on planning trajectories that keep important objects in view so the robot doesn't lose track of hazards it has already seen.

Currently working on a simulation where unseen space starts out unsafe instead of safe.

Sheet 3 of 10

Image-based detection of salt stress in hydroponic tomatoes

Type
NSF REU, University of Wyoming
Advisor
Prof. Liping Wang
Output
GreenSys2027 abstract, first author
Status
Abstract submitted
Open full write-up

During an NSF REU with Prof. Liping Wang, I built a program that looks at photos of tomato plants grown in salty water and estimates how much salt each plant was given, by outlining the plant in the image and comparing its color, texture and shape against plants grown without salt. Across 149 plants its estimates ranked the plants almost the same way the real salt levels did, with a rank correlation of 0.921, and I'm first author on the abstract we submitted to GreenSys2027. The outlining step scored very well overall, but it made most of its mistakes on damaged leaves, which are exactly the part that shows the stress.
